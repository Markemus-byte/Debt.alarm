# Politikeransvar: hvorfor får ikke politikere konsekvenser når de bommer med milliarder?

*Versjon 1, november 2026. En egen studie ved siden av inkassosporet. English version below.*

Spørsmålet: En entreprenør som bommer med en halv milliard på en offentlig kontrakt, må ta tapet selv. Når politikere vedtar prosjekter som sprekker med milliarder, er det innbyggerne som betaler. Hvilket ansvar har politikere egentlig, hva skjedde i en kjent sak fra Oslo, og hva kan endres?

Alle fakta er hentet fra kildene nederst og kontrollert 2. og 3. november 2026. Lovdata lot seg ikke åpne, så teksten i straffeloven §§ 171–173 er gjengitt etter Store norske leksikon, ikke etter lovteksten. Det står ved hvert slikt punkt. Studien er skrevet og kontrollert med hjelp av en AI (Claude), ikke av en jurist.

## Hvorfor dette hører hjemme i Debt.alarm

Debt.alarm handler om regler som slår hardest mot folk som ikke klarer å svare. [Rettskartet](rettskart.md) viser at den som ikke svarer namsmannen eller Skatteetaten innen to til tre uker, får trekket beregnet med standardsatser for boutgifter i stedet for det faktiske boliglånet. Fristene og satsene er bestemt av politikere og myndigheter. Når slike regler fører til at folk mister boligen, er spørsmålet det samme som i denne studien: hvem har ansvaret når et offentlig vedtak slår feil, og hva skjer med dem?

## Kort oppsummert

- **Politikere står ikke over loven.** Vanlig straffelov gjelder dem. En vararepresentant på Stortinget ble i 2019 dømt for grovt bedrageri mot Stortinget. Men å legge fram et dårlig kostnadsoverslag eller ta en dårlig beslutning er ikke straffbart, verken for politikere eller for private.
- **Lan Marie Berg fikk en konsekvens.** Oslo bystyre vedtok mistillit mot henne 16. juni 2021, og hele byrådet måtte gå av. Grunnen var at hun holdt tilbake opplysninger om sprekken i omtrent fem måneder, ikke selve regnefeilen.
- **Samme lov gir ulik risiko.** Lederen av renovasjonsselskapet i Veireno-saken fikk 120 dagers fengsel for brudd på arbeidsmiljøloven i Oslo. Da Oslo kommunes egne etater ble tatt for langt flere brudd, ble sanksjonen en varslet bot til kommunen, betalt av skattebetalerne.
- **Det som mangler, er økonomisk risiko.** En entreprenør med fastpris bærer tapet selv. En politiker mister i verste fall vervet, og velgerne kan gi det tilbake ved neste valg.
- **Det som virker best mot sprekker, er ikke straff.** Det er uavhengig kvalitetssikring før vedtak og en plikt til å varsle raskt. Staten har hatt slik kvalitetssikring siden 2000. Kommunene er ikke omfattet.
- **En konkret vei videre er et innbyggerforslag i Oslo.** 300 underskrifter er nok til at bystyret må behandle saken.

## 4. Entreprenøren til sammenligning

Eksempelet med entreprenøren peker på en reell forskjell, men den handler om penger, ikke om straff.

- **Fastpris:** entreprenøren «har den fulle risikoen for lønnsomheten». Bommer firmaet med en halv milliard, taper firmaet den halve milliarden ([CMS, Praktisk entrepriserett](https://cms.law/no/nor/publikasjon/praktisk-entrepriserett/vederlagsformater)).
- **Enhetspris:** byggherren har risikoen for mengdene, entreprenøren for at prisen per enhet holder (samme kilde). Når en offentlig kontrakt er bygget slik, havner mye av risikoen hos det offentlige likevel.
- **Personen bak:** eierne av et aksjeselskap «har ikke personlig ansvar for selskapets forpliktelser eller gjeld». Styremedlemmer kan bli erstatningsansvarlige for skade de forsettlig eller uaktsomt påfører selskapet eller andre ([SNL](https://snl.no/aksjeselskap)).
- **Konkurs:** tingretten kan ilegge konkurskarantene i to år hvis personen har vist seg «uskikka» til styreverv. Det skjer ikke automatisk ([SNL](https://snl.no/konkurskarantene)).
- **Straff:** bare ved juks, for eksempel bevisst uriktig regnskap eller bedrageri. En ærlig regnefeil gir ingen straff.

Entreprenøren har altså egne penger på spill hver gang, mens politikeren ikke har det. Men verken entreprenøren eller politikeren går i fengsel for en ærlig feil. Store fastpriskontrakter ender også ofte i krangel og tilleggskrav ([Teknisk Ukeblad, 2010](https://www.tu.no/artikler/jakten-pa-fastpris-har-gatt-for-langt/252810)), så det offentlige betaler ofte en del av sprekken likevel.

**Politiker og bedriftsleder side om side.** Forskjellen avhenger av om det er snakk om en ærlig feilvurdering eller et lovbrudd.

| Situasjon | Politiker (byråd eller statsråd) | Leder i et privat aksjeselskap |
|---|---|---|
| Ærlig feilvurdering, for eksempel en budsjettsprekk | Politisk: kan miste vervet ved mistillit eller valg. Ingen bot eller straff. | Selskapet bærer tapet. Personen hefter normalt ikke selv ([SNL](https://snl.no/aksjeselskap)). Ingen straff. |
| Holder tilbake opplysninger | Statsråd: kan straffes i riksrett. Byråd: bare politiske følger. | Styremedlemmer kan bli erstatningsansvarlige for skade de forsettlig eller uaktsomt påfører selskapet eller andre ([SNL](https://snl.no/aksjeselskap)). |
| Brudd på arbeidsmiljøloven i virksomheten | Kommunen som arbeidsgiver kan få bot ([Kommunal Rapport](https://www.konsek.no/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/0005-Vedlegg-Ogsa-kommuner-kan-fa-foretaksstraff.pdf)). Vi har ikke funnet noen sak der en byråd personlig er straffet. | Lederen kan straffes med bot eller fengsel inntil ett år, tre år ved særlig skjerpende omstendigheter (arbeidsmiljøloven § 19-1, slik Høyesterett gjengir den i [HR-2020-358-A](https://www.domstol.no/globalassets/upload/hret/avgjorelser/2020/februar-2020/hr-2020-358-a.pdf)). Selskapet kan få foretaksstraff ([SNL](https://snl.no/foretaksstraff)). |
| Bedrageri, korrupsjon, utroskap | Straffes som alle andre. | Straffes. |
| Karriere etterpå | Kan stille til valg og ta nye jobber. | Kan fortsette, med mindre tingretten ilegger konkurskarantene i to år ([SNL](https://snl.no/konkurskarantene)). |

Tabellen viser at en privat leder bærer en personlig straffrisiko for lovbrudd i egen virksomhet som en politiker på toppen av en kommune ikke gjør i praksis. For en ærlig feilvurdering er det derimot verken fengsel eller bot på noen av sidene.

## 5. Samme lov, samme by: Veireno og kommunens egne etater

Renovasjonen i Oslo gir det tydeligste eksempelet på forskjellen.

**Det private selskapet.** Høyesterett dømte 12. februar 2020 daglig leder og styreleder i renovasjonsselskapet som hadde søppelkontrakten med Oslo fra 2015, i media kjent som Veireno-saken ([NRK via SKUP](https://www.skup.no/sites/default/files/2021-01/Lovbruddene%20i%20Oslo%20kommune%20II.pdf)). Han ble dømt for ikke å ha organisert driften slik at reglene om overtid og arbeidsfri kunne følges ([Høyesterett](https://www.domstol.no/en/supremecourt/rulings/2020/supreme-court-criminal-cases/hr-2020-358-a/)). Dommen er anonymisert, og vi navngir ikke personen.

- Arbeidstilsynet fant 354 brudd i oktober 2016 og 231 brudd fra desember 2016 til januar 2017 i Oslo. Selskapet mistet en dispensasjon i februar 2017 fordi det «gjentatte ganger» hadde brutt vilkårene. En ansatt arbeidet 88 timer i den første uken av 2017 ([HR-2020-358-A](https://www.domstol.no/globalassets/upload/hret/avgjorelser/2020/februar-2020/hr-2020-358-a.pdf)).
- Tingretten ga ett år og tre måneders fengsel, lagmannsretten ni måneder og Høyesterett 120 dager. Høyesterett ville gitt fem måneder, men trakk fra for lang saksbehandlingstid. Allmennpreventive hensyn talte for fengsel, selv om det ikke var brukt i lignende saker før ([Høyesterett](https://www.domstol.no/en/supremecourt/rulings/2020/supreme-court-criminal-cases/hr-2020-358-a/)).
- Morselskapet fikk 4,5 millioner kroner i bot i tingretten, men ble frifunnet i lagmannsretten. Driftsselskapet gikk konkurs 21. februar 2017, før straffesaken ([HR-2020-358-A](https://www.domstol.no/globalassets/upload/hret/avgjorelser/2020/februar-2020/hr-2020-358-a.pdf)).

**Kommunens egne etater.** Fra 2019 avslørte NRK brudd på arbeidsmiljøloven i kommunens egne etater ([NRK via SKUP, januar 2021](https://www.skup.no/sites/default/files/2021-01/Lovbruddene%20i%20Oslo%20kommune%20II.pdf)):

- Saken startet med 557 brudd i Energigjenvinningsetaten, rapportert av Bergs byrådsavdeling i august 2019. Bergs egen pressemelding oppga senere 2 911 brudd i hennes etater i 2019.
- I hele kommunen vokste tallet til over 250 000 registrerte brudd, blant annet om lag 65 000 i Sykehjemsetaten siden 2013. Mange av disse etatene lå under andre byråder enn Berg.
- Arbeidstilsynet varslet kommunen om 900 000 kroner i bot etter to tilsyn. Politiet skulle i januar 2021 avgjøre om de ville åpne egen etterforskning. Vi har ikke funnet hva de besluttet.

**Hvorfor så ulikt?** Arbeidsmiljølovens straffebud retter seg mot arbeidsgiveren og den som leder virksomheten. I det private er det eieren og daglig leder. I kommunen er arbeidsgiveren kommunen selv, og da blir sanksjonen en bot som skattebetalerne betaler. Høyesterett la vekt på at bruddene var «forsettlige og systematiske» og fortsatte etter pålegg fra Arbeidstilsynet ([HR-2020-358-A](https://www.domstol.no/globalassets/upload/hret/avgjorelser/2020/februar-2020/hr-2020-358-a.pdf)). Om en byråd eller etatsdirektør kunne vært straffet personlig, har vi ikke funnet noen vurdering av. Det står som et åpent spørsmål.

Granskingsutvalget (NOU 2020: 9 «Blindsonen») fant en systemsvikt. | Statsråd Anniken Hauglie gikk ut av regjeringen i januar 2020, mens et mistillitsforslag mot henne var varslet. Vi har ikke funnet at noen ble straffeforfulgt. | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Social_Security_scandal), [SNL](https://snl.no/Anniken_Hauglie) |

**Store kostnadssprekker uten personlig ansvar:**

- *Nytt regjeringskvartal, byggetrinn 1:* Stortinget vedtok en kostnadsramme på 20,5 milliarder kroner i desember 2020. Den ble økt til 23,7 milliarder i 2023. Riksrevisjonen skrev i juni 2023 at det var uvisst om rammen ville holde, og at byggetrinnet ble gjennomført med mindre omfang enn forutsatt ([Riksrevisjonen](https://www.riksrevisjonen.no/globalassets/rapporter/NO-2022-2023/gjennomforingen-av-byggetrinn-1-nytt-regjeringskvartal.pdf)).
- *Fornebubanen:* Styrings- og kostnadsrammen var 16,2 og 18,5 milliarder 2018-kroner i 2019. I dag er kostnadsrammen 31,2 milliarder og styringsrammen 27,5 milliarder løpende kroner ([Kommunerevisjonen, rapport 14/2024](https://www.oslo.kommune.no/politikk/budsjett-regnskap-og-rapportering/rapporter-fra-kommunerevisjonen/rapport-14-2024-kontraktsoppfolging-i-fornebubanen/)). I november 2025 meldte selskapet at kostnadene vil gå over styringsrammen ([Teknisk Ukeblad](https://www.tu.no/artikler/forsinkelser-i-byggingen-av-fornebubanen-og-kostnadssprekk/564452)). Kommunerevisjonen mente likevel at systemet for kostnadskontroll var «godt innarbeidet».
- *Viken:* Fylket ble opprettet 1. januar 2020 og oppløst 1. januar 2024 ([SNL](https://snl.no/Viken_-_tidligere_fylke)). Staten foreslo 176,9 millioner kroner for å dekke delingskostnadene for Viken, Vestfold og Telemark, Troms og Finnmark og Ålesund til sammen ([Regjeringen](https://www.regjeringen.no/no/aktuelt/regjeringa-dekker-delingskostandene-for-fylker-og-kommuner/id3015889/)). Vi har ikke funnet en sikker kilde for hva Viken kostet alene.

Her er det verdt å merke seg at sprekker ikke alltid skyldes dårlig kontroll. Prisveksten i 2021–2022 rammet både Fornebubanen og vannprosjektet. Det er et argument for forslag A og C nedenfor: realistiske anslag og åpne tall, slik at velgerne kan se forskjell på uflaks og dårlig styring.

## 7. Andre land

**Danmark: ministre kan dømmes.** Rigsretten dømmer ministre som bryter grunnloven, loven eller pliktene som minister. Tidligere justisminister Erik Ninn-Hansen fikk fire måneders betinget fengsel i Tamilsaken i 1995. Inger Støjberg fikk 60 dagers ubetinget fengsel 13. desember 2021 for en ulovlig instruks om å skille asylpar der den ene var under 18 år ([Lex.dk](https://lex.dk/Rigsretten), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impeachment_of_Inger_St%C3%B8jberg)). Begge sakene gjaldt lovbrudd, ikke kostnadssprekker.

**Sverige: mulig, men nesten aldri brukt.** En statsråd kan bare dømmes for noe gjort i tjenesten hvis hun eller han «grovt har åsidosatt sin tjänsteplikt» (regeringsformen 13 kap. 3 §). Riksdagens konstitusjonsutskott bestemmer om det skal tas ut tiltale, og bestemmelsen ble sist brukt på 1800-tallet ([Lawline, 2022](https://lawline.se/answers/vad-kravs-for-att-atal-av-statsrad-ska-vackas)).

**Storbritannia: lokalpolitikere måtte betale selv, men ordningen ble fjernet.** Før år 2000 kunne lokalpolitikere i England pålegges å betale tilbake penger brukt ulovlig, kalt «surcharge» ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surcharge_(sanction))). Det mest kjente eksempelet er Shirley Porter i Westminster: i 1996 ble hun og fem andre gjort ansvarlige for 36,1 millioner pund for å ha brukt salg av kommuneboliger til å vinne velgere. House of Lords ga revisoren medhold i 2001, og i 2004 inngikk hun forlik på 12,3 millioner pund ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homes_for_votes_scandal)). Ordningen ble avskaffet med Local Government Act 2000 ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2000/22/notes/division/1/5/1)) etter råd fra Committee on Standards in Public Life, som mente at beløpene ikke sto i forhold til evnen til å betale eller graden av skyld. Storbritannia har i tillegg straffbudet «misconduct in public office», som også gjelder folkevalgte ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misconduct_in_public_office)).

**Sveits: folket har siste ord over store utgifter.** Mange kantoner og kommuner har finansreferendum. Over en viss sum må utgiften godkjennes ved folkeavstemning; i byen Aarau gjaldt det over seks millioner franc i 2017. Forskere finner lavere utgifter per innbygger der ordningen finnes, men den forsinker også prosjekter, noen ganger med år ([Swissinfo, 2017](https://www.swissinfo.ch/ger/wirtschaft/schauplatz-schweiz-10-_das-letzte-wort-haben-die-buerger-auch-beim-geld/43151852)).

**Norge selv: kvalitetssikring i staten.** Etter mange sprekker innførte staten i 2000 ekstern kvalitetssikring (KS1 og KS2) av store prosjekter. Den gjelder i dag statlige prosjekter over én milliard kroner, eller 300 millioner for digitalisering ([SNL](https://snl.no/Statens_prosjektmodell), [Concept, NTNU](https://www.ntnu.no/concept/bakgrunn-for-ordningen)). I en studie av 40 prosjekter endte 80 prosent under kostnadsrammen, og i 31 veiprosjekter var sluttkostnaden i snitt lik forventet kostnad ([Welde, Concept 2014](https://www.ntnu.no/documents/1261860271/1262021752/Kostnadsutvikling+i+vegprosjekter.pdf/34cd4f09-c2ff-4b19-9aab-4fd032c1811a?version=1.0), som siterer Samset og Volden 2013). Ordningen gjelder ikke kommunene, selv om flere har laget egne modeller etter samme mønster ([SNL](https://snl.no/Statens_prosjektmodell)).

## 8. Hvorfor sprekker store prosjekter?

Forskningen peker mer på systemet enn på enkeltpersoner. Mennesker undervurderer kostnad og tid og overvurderer nytten av egne planer ([Wikipedia: Reference class forecasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reference_class_forecasting)). Forskning som Bent Flyvbjerg har ledet, regner slik optimisme som en av de største enkeltårsakene til sprekker i store prosjekter ([Wikipedia: Optimism bias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimism_bias)). Derfor virker kvalitetssikring utenfra: noen uten egeninteresse sammenligner med hva lignende prosjekter faktisk kostet.

Straff for selve regnefeilen gir derfor trolig lite. Det som kan sanksjoneres med mening, er å skjule et kjent problem, slik bystyret mente skjedde i 2021.

## 10. Hvordan gå fram

1. **Velg mål.** Færre sprekker, ærligere informasjon eller strengere straff? Vi anbefaler de to første, fordi det er der tiltakene har dokumentert effekt.
2. **Samle tall.** Lag en liste over Oslos største prosjekter de siste 10–15 årene med første vedtatte ramme og sluttkostnad. Kilder er bystyrets budsjettvedtak og Kommunerevisjonens rapporter.
3. **Snakk med fagfolk.** Kontrollutvalget og Kommunerevisjonen i Oslo, forskningsprogrammet Concept ved NTNU (som evaluerer statens kvalitetssikring), Skattebetalerforeningen og en jurist som kan svare på det strafferettslige.
4. **Lag et innbyggerforslag i Oslo.** Kommuneloven § 12-1 sier at kommunestyret må ta stilling til et forslag hvis minst to prosent av innbyggerne står bak, og at «300 underskrifter i kommunen» alltid er nok ([Regjeringen](https://www.regjeringen.no/no/dokumenter/-12-1-sporsmal-om-innbyggerforslag-underskrift-som-tilhorer-avdod-person/id2965488/)). Et forslag om A, B og C er konkret og gjennomførbart.
5. **Gå nasjonalt etterpå.** Ta forslaget til Stortingets kommunal- og forvaltningskomité, slik at kvalitetssikring og varslingsplikt kan skrives inn i kommuneloven for alle kommuner.

# English version: Why don't politicians face consequences when budgets miss by billions?

*Version 1, November 2026. A separate study beside the debt-collection track. Sources are listed in the Norwegian version above.*

The question: a contractor who misjudges a public contract by half a billion carries the loss. When politicians approve projects that overrun by billions, residents pay. What accountability do politicians actually have, what happened in a well-known Oslo case, and what could change?

All facts come from the sources above and were checked on 2 and 3 November 2026. Lovdata could not be opened, so Penal Code §§ 171–173 are described from the Store norske leksikon encyclopaedia, not the statute. The study was written and checked with the help of an AI (Claude), not by a lawyer.

## Why this belongs in Debt.alarm

Debt.alarm is about rules that hit hardest at people who cannot respond. The [legal map](rettskart.md) shows that someone who does not answer the enforcement officer or the Tax Administration within two to three weeks gets their deduction calculated with standard housing-cost rates instead of the actual mortgage. Those deadlines and rates are set by politicians and authorities. When such rules make people lose their homes, the question is the same as in this study: who is responsible when a public decision goes wrong, and what happens to them?

## Summary

- **Politicians are not above the law.** Ordinary criminal law applies. A deputy member of the Storting was convicted of aggravated fraud against the Storting in 2019. But a bad cost estimate or a bad decision is not a crime, for politicians or for private people.
- **Lan Marie Berg did face a consequence.** Oslo's city council passed a vote of no confidence on 16 June 2021 and the whole city government resigned. The reason was that she held back information about the overrun for about five months, not the estimating error itself.
- **The same law carries different risk.** The manager of the waste company in the Veireno case got 120 days in prison for Working Environment Act breaches in Oslo. When Oslo's own agencies were caught with far more breaches, the sanction was a fine notice to the municipality, paid by taxpayers.
- **What is missing is financial risk.** A contractor on a fixed price carries the loss. A politician at worst loses the post, and voters can hand it back at the next election.
- **What works best against overruns is not punishment.** It is independent quality assurance before decisions and a duty to report early. The state has had such quality assurance since 2000. Municipalities are not covered.
- **A concrete next step is a citizens' proposal in Oslo.** 300 signatures are enough to make the council consider it.

## 1. The Lan Marie Berg case

Lan Marie Berg (Green Party, MDG) was Oslo's city commissioner for environment and transport from October 2015. The case concerned the Water and Sewerage Agency's project for a new water supply from Holsfjorden, in 2021 described as a new backup water supply. The timeline also includes an earlier case about breaches of the Working Environment Act, because it was about the same thing: whether the council was told enough, in time.

| When | What happened |
|---|---|
| 2019–2020 | An earlier case: PwC found 807 breaches of the Working Environment Act in the Waste-to-Energy Agency from January 2017 to March 2019, mostly on working hours. Berg ordered the review after anonymous whistleblowing. The opposition demanded an investigation of Berg and the agency in September 2019. |
| November–December 2020 | The Progress Party and FNB table a no-confidence motion over the breaches and over withholding information from the council. It does not pass. |
| December 2020 | Berg learns the project will cost far more than the council approved in 2019, but does not tell the council. |
| 20 May 2021 | The council learns of the overrun in the revised budget: NOK 17.7 billion, 5.2 billion more than previously estimated. The 2019 cost frame was 12.5 billion and is to become 17.7; the control frame is to go from 10.9 to 15.7 billion. |
| 15 June 2021 | The Red Party announces support for the Progress Party's motion, securing a majority: "The environment commissioner has broken the duty to inform the council." |
| 16 June 2021 | The motion passes, backed by FNB, Conservatives, Christian Democrats, Liberals, Centre Party and Red Party. Governing mayor Raymond Johansen had made it a confidence matter, so the whole city government resigned and stayed on as caretaker. |
| 18 June 2021 | Berg leaves. Hanna Marcussen acts, then Sirin Hellvin Stav takes over. Johansen is asked to form a new city government. |
| Autumn 2021 | Berg is elected to the Storting and serves one term, 2021–2025. She did not seek re-election. |
| May 2026 | Altinget reports that Berg, now a member of the Restructuring Commission (Omstillingskommisjonen), will become director for climate action, peace and democracy at the aid organisation Norcap from August 2026. |
| March 2023 | NOK 3.5–3.7 billion more, mainly from wage and price growth; construction costs rose over 25 % in 2021–2022. |
| August 2024 | Another 2.8 billion, now for the Huseby treatment plant. The agency director: "the cost calculations for this sub-project have not been good enough". The control frame was then about 26.5 billion and the cost frame 30.2 billion. |

Berg argued she could not give the council correct figures until the agency's calculations had been quality-checked by someone outside the municipality. The council majority held that it was not up to a commissioner to decide when elected members get to know.

How long the council was kept out depends on where one counts from. NTB says she learned of the overrun in December 2020, about five months before the council was told. An opinion piece in Nettavisen argues it was 14 months from the first signs, and that the budget burst with the first contract in March 2020.

We found nothing indicating that Berg was investigated, charged or punished in either case. The consequences were political.

**Note:** the 2021 frames (15.7 and 17.7 billion) and the 2023–2024 frames (around 26.5–30.2 billion) come from different reports and may rest on different scope and price levels. We could not reconcile them.

## 2. Does the premise hold?

| Claim | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "She gets zero punishment, zero consequences." | Partly | She lost her post and the whole city government had to resign, the strongest sanction the council has. But there was no financial or criminal consequence, and voters elected her to the Storting the same autumn. She also survived a 2020 no-confidence motion over the Working Environment Act breaches. |
| "Politicians have the right to commit serious crimes." | Does not hold | Criminal law applies to politicians like everyone else. What is not a crime is a bad decision or a missed estimate, for private people too. |
| "She sits and calculates cost estimates." | Partly | Estimates are made by the agency and consultants. Her duty was to steer and keep the council informed, and that duty is what she was brought down over. |
| "Residents are the ones who pay." | Holds | Mainly through the water and sewerage fee. The August 2024 increase alone adds about NOK 380 a year for an average home from 2028. |
| "A contractor who missed by half a billion would be punished." | Partly | The contractor would lose money and might go bankrupt. But the person behind a limited company is normally not personally liable, and an honest miscalculation is not a crime. |

## 3. What accountability exists today?

| Mechanism | Who it covers | Trigger | How often used |
|---|---|---|---|
| Elections | All elected officials | Every four years | Always. The main sanction in a democracy. |
| No confidence | The government, and city governments in parliamentary municipalities such as Oslo and Bergen | Majority in the Storting or city council | Rare, but works. The Berg case is an example. |
| Control committee and municipal audit | Municipal administration and city government | The committee orders reviews; its chair cannot be from the mayor's party (Local Government Act § 23-1) | Ongoing. Oslo's audit office has 44 staff. |
| Auditor General and Storting control committee | The state, not municipalities | Annual audits and own cases | Ongoing |
| Court of impeachment (Riksrett) | Ministers, Storting members, Supreme Court judges | Crimes in office, including withholding or giving false information to the Storting | Last used 1926–27; acquitted because the case was time-barred. |
| Penal Code, misconduct in office (§§ 171–173) | Public officials | Gross breaches of official duty and abuse of power; up to six years (per SNL; statute not read) | We found no cases against elected officials for political decisions. |
| Ordinary criminal law | Everyone | Fraud, corruption, breach of trust | Used. Mazyar Keshvari (Progress Party), a deputy member of the Storting, was convicted of aggravated fraud against the Storting in 2019. |
| Personal financial liability | No scheme found | – | We found no rule making an elected official personally liable for a decision or an overrun. |

The key point: impeachment and the Responsibility Act, which can punish a minister for misleading the Storting, apply only at state level. An Oslo commissioner has a similar duty in the city government's rules (§ 3-1), but it has no deadline, and breaking it has only political consequences.

## 4. The contractor by comparison

- **Fixed price:** the contractor "carries the full risk for profitability". Miss by half a billion and the company loses half a billion.
- **Unit price:** the client carries quantity risk; the contractor carries the risk that the unit price is enough. Under such contracts much of the risk lands on the public anyway.
- **The person behind it:** shareholders have no personal liability for the company's debts. Board members can be liable for damage they cause intentionally or negligently.
- **Bankruptcy:** a court may impose a two-year ban on directorships if the person has proven unfit. It is not automatic.
- **Punishment:** only for cheating, such as false accounts or fraud. An honest miscalculation is not punished.

So the contractor always has money at stake, and the politician does not. But neither goes to prison for an honest mistake. Large fixed-price contracts also often end in disputes and extra claims, so the public often pays part of the overrun anyway.

**Politician and business leader side by side.** The difference depends on whether it is an honest misjudgment or a breach of law.

| Situation | Politician (city commissioner or minister) | Leader of a private limited company |
|---|---|---|
| Honest misjudgment, such as a budget overrun | Political: may lose the post through no confidence or elections. No fine or punishment. | The company carries the loss. The person is normally not liable. No punishment. |
| Withholding information | Minister: can be punished by the impeachment court. City commissioner: only political consequences. | Board members can be liable for damage they cause intentionally or negligently. |
| Working Environment Act breaches in the organisation | The municipality as employer can be fined. We found no case where a commissioner was personally punished. | The leader can be fined or jailed for up to one year, three years in especially aggravating circumstances (§ 19-1 as quoted by the Supreme Court). The company can get a corporate fine. |
| Fraud, corruption, breach of trust | Punished like everyone else. | Punished. |
| Career afterwards | Can stand for election and take new jobs. | Can carry on unless a court imposes a two-year ban on directorships. |

So a private leader carries a personal criminal risk for breaches in their own business that a politician at the top of a municipality in practice does not. For an honest misjudgment there is neither prison nor a fine on either side.

## 5. Same law, same city: Veireno and the municipality's own agencies

**The private company.** On 12 February 2020 the Supreme Court convicted the general manager and chair of the waste company that held Oslo's collection contract from 2015, known in the media as the Veireno case, for not organising the work so that overtime and rest rules could be followed. The judgment is anonymised and we do not name the person. The Labour Inspection found 354 breaches in October 2016 and 231 from December 2016 to January 2017 in Oslo; one employee worked 88 hours in the first week of 2017. The district court gave one year and three months, the appeal court nine months, and the Supreme Court 120 days (five months, reduced for long proceedings), citing general deterrence. The parent company was fined NOK 4.5 million in the district court but acquitted on appeal. The operating company went bankrupt on 21 February 2017, before the criminal case.

**The municipality's own agencies.** From 2019 NRK revealed breaches in its own agencies. It began with 557 breaches in the Waste-to-Energy Agency, reported by Berg's department in August 2019; Berg's own press release later gave 2,911 breaches in her agencies in 2019. Across the municipality the count grew to over 250,000 registered breaches, including about 65,000 in the Nursing Home Agency since 2013, many of them under other commissioners. The Labour Inspection gave notice of a NOK 900,000 fine. In January 2021 the police were deciding whether to investigate; we have not found the outcome.

**Why so different?** The Act's penal provision targets the employer and whoever runs the business. In a private company that is the owner and manager. In the municipality the employer is the municipality itself, so the sanction becomes a fine paid by taxpayers. The Supreme Court stressed that the breaches were intentional and systematic and continued after orders from the Labour Inspection. We found no assessment of whether a commissioner or agency director could have been punished personally.

## 6. Other Norwegian cases

| Case | What happened | Consequences |
|---|---|---|
| Kings Bay, 1962–63 | 21 miners died in an explosion at the state-owned coal company in Ny-Ålesund on 5 November 1962; 43 had died in explosions in 1948–1953. The inquiry criticised the company and the Labour Inspection for neglecting safety over a long time. | Industry minister Kjell Holler resigned. The Gerhardsen government fell 76 to 74 in August 1963. The Director of Public Prosecutions decided no one would be charged. |
| NH90 helicopters, 2001–2022 | 14 helicopters ordered in 2001 for delivery by 2008; by 2018 only half were delivered. The Auditor General found it "strongly reprehensible" that the ministry and the Armed Forces did not examine the decision basis more thoroughly, and put a substantial part of the blame for delays on the supplier. | The contract was ended in 2022. We found no one held personally responsible. |
| NAV/EEA scandal, 2019 | Made public on 28 October 2019. At least 80 people wrongly convicted of benefit fraud; at least 2,400 wrongly asked to repay. The inquiry (NOU 2020: 9) found a system failure. | Minister Anniken Hauglie left the government in January 2020 while a no-confidence motion had been announced. We found no prosecutions. |

**Large overruns without personal responsibility:** the government quarter's first stage went from a NOK 20.5 billion cost frame (December 2020) to 23.7 billion (2023), and the Auditor General said in June 2023 that it was uncertain whether it would hold and that the scope had been reduced. Fornebubanen's frames went from 16.2 and 18.5 billion 2018-kroner (2019) to a 31.2 billion cost frame and a 27.5 billion control frame today, and in November 2025 the company said costs would exceed the control frame; the city's audit office still found cost control "well established". Viken county existed from 2020 to 2024; the state proposed NOK 176.9 million to cover splitting costs for four counties and municipalities together. Overruns are not always poor control: price growth in 2021–2022 hit both Fornebubanen and the water project, which is an argument for proposals A and C below.

## 7. Other countries

**Denmark:** the Court of Impeachment tries ministers. Erik Ninn-Hansen got four months' suspended prison in the Tamil case in 1995. Inger Støjberg got 60 days' unconditional prison on 13 December 2021 for an unlawful order to separate asylum-seeking couples where one partner was under 18. Both cases were about breaking the law, not cost overruns.

**Sweden:** a minister can only be convicted for acts in office if they "grossly neglected their duty". The Constitution Committee decides whether to prosecute; the rule was last used in the 1800s.

**United Kingdom:** before 2000, English councillors could be "surcharged" to repay money spent unlawfully. In the best-known case, Shirley Porter and five others were held liable in 1996 for £36.1 million for using council-home sales to win votes; the House of Lords backed the auditor in 2001 and she settled for £12.3 million in 2004. The Local Government Act 2000 abolished surcharge after the Committee on Standards in Public Life found it bore no relation to ability to pay or culpability. The UK also has the criminal offence of misconduct in public office, which covers elected officials.

**Switzerland:** many cantons and municipalities have a financial referendum. Spending above a threshold needs voter approval; in Aarau the threshold was CHF 6 million in 2017. Studies find lower spending where it exists, but it also delays projects.

**Norway itself:** the state introduced external quality assurance (KS1 and KS2) in 2000. It now covers state projects above NOK 1 billion, or 300 million for digital projects. In one study of 40 projects, 80 % ended below the cost frame; in 31 road projects the final cost on average equalled the expected cost. Municipalities are not covered, though several have their own versions.

## 8. Why do big projects overrun?

Research points more at the system than at individuals. People underestimate cost and time and overestimate the benefits of their own plans. Research led by Bent Flyvbjerg counts this optimism among the biggest single causes of megaproject overspend. That is why outside quality assurance works: someone with no stake compares with what similar projects actually cost. Punishing the miscalculation itself probably achieves little. What can meaningfully be sanctioned is hiding a known problem.

## 9. Possible solutions

| | Proposal | For | Against | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | **External quality assurance** for municipal projects above, say, NOK 500 million, modelled on the state's KS2 | Proven in the state; tackles the cause; Oslo can probably adopt it alone | Costs money and time; needs a law change to cover all municipalities | Recommended |
| B | **Duty to report with a deadline**, say 30 days, when an estimate exceeds the frame by a set percentage; breaches go to the control committee | Targets exactly what went wrong in the Berg case; cheap | Needs careful definitions; still only political consequences | Recommended |
| C | **Open project register** for projects above, say, NOK 100 million, with original and current estimates, updated quarterly | Cheap; gives voters facts before elections | Figures must be price-adjusted consistently; no sanction in itself | Recommended |
| D | **Criminal liability for withholding information**, also for city commissioners, only when intentional or grossly negligent | Same rule locally as nationally; Denmark shows it can work | Prosecutions as a political weapon; Norway's impeachment court has not sat for almost a century | Consider |
| E | **Referendum on large spending**, Swiss model | Residents decide directly; research suggests lower spending | Delays; a big break with Norwegian practice, where local referendums are advisory | Consider |
| F | **Personal financial liability or bans** for politicians after big overruns | Gives politicians money at stake | The UK abolished it; deters ordinary people from standing; hits whoever voted, not whoever miscalculated; a ban takes the choice from voters | Not recommended |

Cheating and fraud are already crimes. Beyond that, we think A, B and C deliver more than punishment.

**The same applies to the enforcement officer.** The idea behind B and C can be used on the rules in the [legal map](rettskart.md): the authorities could be required to count and publish how many deductions are calculated with standard rates because the debtor did not answer, and how many of those later end in a forced sale of the home. That gives politicians a number to answer for.

## 10. How to go forward

1. **Pick the goal:** fewer overruns, more honest information, or tougher punishment. We recommend the first two.
2. **Collect figures:** Oslo's largest projects of the last 10–15 years, first approved frame and final cost.
3. **Talk to experts:** Oslo's control committee and audit office, the Concept programme at NTNU, the Norwegian Taxpayers' Association, and a lawyer for the criminal-law question.
4. **File a citizens' proposal in Oslo:** under the Local Government Act § 12-1 the council must consider a proposal backed by 2 % of residents, and 300 signatures are always enough.
5. **Then go national:** take it to the Storting's local government committee so quality assurance and the reporting duty can be written into the Local Government Act.
